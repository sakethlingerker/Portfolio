// 01 · PROBLEM SCOPE & TECHNICAL CONTRIBUTIONS

Multimodal Multi-Video Question Answering

Bridging raw acoustic signal segmentation with downstream ASR, transformer-based QA, and video synthesis.

The Challenge

Noisy Lecture Streams & Intra-Word Dropouts

Querying large educational video archives requires accurate acoustic segmentation before transcription. I observed that raw lecture audio contains instructor pauses, classroom reverberation, and brief unvoiced stops.

Standard energy detectors either trigger false voice flags on ambient noise (causing ASR hallucinations) or suffer intra-speech dropouts that clip words mid-sentence.

My Responsibilities & Impact

My Technical Contributions

  • Python VAD Modules: I developed frame-level evaluation pipelines (10 ms frame shift) for our AI-driven video summarization system.
  • Short-Gap Recovery Algorithm: I designed a post-processing filter to bridge short 0-runs between active speech, lifting overall accuracy from 91.54% to 96.02%.
  • WhisperX Forced Alignment: I mapped word-level timestamps from base (1,877 words) and large-v3 (1,884 words) to 10 ms rVAD frames.
  • Cross-Module Collaboration: I collaborated with the Speech Lab team across our four core pillars: Voice Activity Detection, Automatic Speech Recognition, Question Answering, and Video Stitching.

Experiment 01 · Frame-Level Post-Processing

Recovering Short Acoustic Dropouts Between Continuous Speech

To mitigate false negatives caused by brief intra-speech pauses, I built a post-processing algorithm that scanned the binary rVAD sequence and flipped consecutive runs of 0-valued frames to 1 if and only if the run length was ≤ my selected threshold N frames (50, 80, or 100 frames at 10 ms shift) and strictly flanked by 1-valued speech frames on both sides.

Raw rVAD Sequence: [1, 1, 1, 0, 0, 0, 1, 1, 1] (Run ≤ N flanked by 1s) → Post-Processed: [1, 1, 1, 1, 1, 1, 1, 1, 1] (Bridged Speech Segment)

50 frames 0.5 s 0.9567 (95.67%) 0.9770 0.9733 0.9807 2,670
80 frames 0.8 s 0.9601 (96.01%) 0.9790 0.9670 0.9912 2,459
100 frames OPTIMAL 1.0 s 0.9602 (96.02%) 0.9791 0.9643 0.9943 2,453

50 frames (0.5 s) 56,635 1,554 1,116 2,372

Precision–Recall Trade-Off Insight: Widening the gap threshold from 50 to 100 frames reduced False Negatives by 70.4% (1,116 → 330) and elevated Recall to 0.9943. This did increase False Positives (1,554 → 2,123) and slightly tapered Precision (0.9733 → 0.9643), but overall Accuracy between 80 and 100 frames plateaued successfully.
Experiment 02 · Phonetic Word Alignment

Word-Level VAD Cross-Validation Using WhisperX

Because SRT subtitles group words into reading blocks, I deployed WhisperX (testing both the base and large-v3 models) to extract exact word-level start and end timestamps, which I then mapped to my 10 ms rVAD frames.

WhisperX Model Word-Level VAD Score Non-Word VAD Score Extracted Word Count SRT Reference Alignment Notes
base 0.978 (97.8%) 0.718 (71.8%) 1,877 Matches SRT reference word count exactly (1,877 words)
large-v3 0.978 (97.8%) 0.719 (71.9%) 1,884 Differs in coverage; I identified 76 missing words relative to my SRT comparison
Model Alignment Takeaway: Both WhisperX models achieved an identical 0.978 word-level VAD score, confirming that my rVAD pipeline reliably captures spoken words. Non-word regions scored 0.718–0.719, demonstrating that inter-word acoustic intervals require separate analysis.
Experiment 03 · Candidate Non-Speech Heuristic

Evaluating Non-Speech Detection via Subtitle Gaps & Boundary Timing

To inspect potential false speech detections outside subtitle blocks, I converted intervals between the end of one subtitle and the start of the next (plus 00:00:00,000 to the first subtitle) to 10 ms frame indices and checked them for 0-valued rVAD frames.

Methodological Context: Since SRT subtitle gaps are grouped for viewer readability rather than acoustic silence, they frequently contain natural breathing or low-amplitude speech. Therefore, I treated this metric as a subtitle-gap-based non-speech analysis rather than absolute ground-truth silence accuracy.
+ View Subtitle-Level Start/End Boundary Check Note (80 / 274 blocks)

In a separate exploratory check, I evaluated subtitle-level boundary alignment, recording a 0.2920 pass ratio (80 of 274 blocks), dependent on strict start/end boundary tolerance rules.
