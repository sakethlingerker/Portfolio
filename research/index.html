Research Scope: Multimodal Multi-Video Question and Answering

1. RESEARCH PROBLEM CONTEXT

In multimodal question-answering systems querying expansive multi-video archives, accurate acoustic segmentation is the foundational prerequisite. Raw video streams—particularly educational lectures—contain substantial non-speech audio including instructor pauses, classroom reverberation, paper rustling, and ambient acoustic noise.

2. THE ENGINEERING BOTTLENECK

Standard acoustic energy detectors routinely trigger false voice flags on non-linguistic noise. These false positives overburden downstream Automatic Speech Recognition (ASR) engines with acoustic artifacts, inflating inference latency and inducing hallucinated transcripts in multimodal question-answering models.

3. FOUR SYSTEM COMPONENTS IDENTIFIED IN CERTIFICATE
Voice Activity Detection (VAD) Automatic Speech Recognition (ASR) Question Answering (QA) Video Stitching

Evaluating & Improving Robust Voice Activity Detection (rVAD 2.0)

A substantial portion of the documented experimentation focused on evaluating and optimizing rVAD 2.0 (robust Voice Activity Detection) on educational lecture audio. A lecture video from Udemy was selected, its audio extracted and converted to a standardized 16 kHz stream, and processed through rVAD. The algorithm generated a frame-level binary sequence where 1 represented speech and 0 represented non-speech, evaluated at a standardized 10 ms frame shift against synchronized SRT subtitle timings.

// EXPERIMENTAL PROCEDURE 01 GAP RECOVERY BENCHMARK

Post-Processing to Recover Short Gaps Between Continuous Speech

To mitigate false negatives caused by brief acoustic dropouts (such as unvoiced stops, short vocal hesitations, or intra-word breathing pauses), a boundary-constrained post-processing algorithm was engineered. The algorithm scanned the binary VAD sequence, identified consecutive runs of 0-valued frames occurring between speech segments, and flipped them to 1 if and only if:

  • The run length of 0s was at or below a selected threshold \(N\) frames.
  • The run was strictly flanked by 1-valued (speech) frames on both preceding and succeeding sides.

Empirical Trade-Off Dynamics: Increasing the maximum gap threshold from 50 to 100 frames slashed false negatives by 70.4% (from 1,116 down to 330) and boosted recall from 0.9807 to 0.9943. Simultaneously, it introduced additional false positives (rising from 1,554 to 2,123), which caused precision to gently taper from 0.9733 to 0.9643. Overall frame-level accuracy and F1 score steadily climbed, with the 100-frame setting yielding the highest recall (0.9943) and F1 score (0.9791), while accuracy between 80 and 100 frames remained virtually equivalent (0.9601 vs 0.9602).
// EXPERIMENTAL PROCEDURE 02 FORCED ALIGNMENT EVALUATION

Word-Level VAD Evaluation Using WhisperX Forced Alignment

To cross-validate speech detection against phonetic word boundaries rather than block subtitles, WhisperX was deployed to extract precise word-level start and end timestamps using both the base and large-v3 models. Each extracted word interval was mapped to its corresponding 10 ms rVAD frames. Word-level VAD coverage was computed as the proportion of frames within each word interval that rVAD labeled as speech. Acoustic frames outside the union of all word intervals were separately quantified as non-word regions.

WhisperX Model Word-Level VAD Score Non-Word VAD Score Extracted Word Count Reference Alignment & Nuance
base 0.978 (97.8%) 0.718 (71.8%) 1,877 Matches SRT reference word count exactly (1,877 words)
large-v3 0.978 (97.8%) 0.719 (71.9%) 1,884 +7 words overall; includes 76 words missing relative to SRT reference comparison
Model Alignment Nuance: Both models produced identical high word-level VAD scores of 0.978, demonstrating that rVAD robustly captures speech across distinct model sizes. Their non-word scores were also tightly clustered (0.718 – 0.719), indicating that inter-word acoustic segments present distinct signal characteristics requiring separate analysis. Crucially, while the base model's extracted word count matched the SRT reference count of 1,877 words exactly, the large-v3 model produced 1,884 words with a reported set of 76 missing words relative to the reference comparison, reflecting different tokenization, hallucination suppression, and decoding behaviors.
// EXPERIMENTAL PROCEDURE 03 CANDIDATE SILENCE INVESTIGATION

Investigating Non-Speech Detection Using Subtitle Gaps & Boundary Timing

A complementary evaluation explored measuring rVAD behavior outside active subtitle intervals. Candidate non-speech intervals were extracted by taking gaps between the end time of one subtitle block and the start time of the subsequent subtitle, together with the leading interval from 00:00:00,000 to the first subtitle. Timestamps were converted to 10 ms frame indices to calculate silence scores (proportion of 0-valued rVAD frames) and inspect potential false speech triggers.

Essential Academic & Evaluation Limitation: Subtitle gaps serve only as an operational heuristic for non-speech analysis, not ground-truth acoustic silence. Subtitles are segmented for human reading pacing and viewer comprehension; a subtitle gap may naturally contain low-amplitude speech, conversational laughter, or instructor breathing pauses. Consequently, this study documents this method as subtitle-gap-based non-speech analysis rather than definitive silence classification accuracy.
SUBTITLE-LEVEL TIMING DIAGNOSTIC NOTE

A separate exploratory boundary check yielded 80 out of 274 subtitle blocks (ratio of 0.2920). This value represents a subtitle-level block-boundary timing pass ratio rather than frame-level or model accuracy. Its interpretation depends strictly on the exact tolerance threshold applied to determine whether subtitle start and end boundaries synchronize with continuous rVAD speech labels.

// EMPIRICAL TAKEAWAYS

Seven Core Research Findings

Key empirical conclusions documented throughout the rVAD 2.0 evaluation and WhisperX comparative alignment experiments.

[01]

91.54% Baseline Frame Accuracy

Initial rVAD 2.0 frame classification against synchronized SRT subtitle references demonstrated robust baseline speech detection on 16 kHz educational lecture audio.

[02]

Short-Gap Recovery Yields ~96.0% Accuracy

Boundary-constrained post-processing of zero-valued gaps between active speech regions elevated frame accuracy to 96.02% and F1 score to 0.9791 at the 100-frame (1.0 s) setting.

[03]

Precision-Recall Trade-off Dynamics

Widening the gap threshold from 50 to 100 frames slashed false negatives by 70.4% (1,116 down to 330) and boosted recall to 0.9943, with a minor trade-off in precision (0.9733 to 0.9643).

[04]

0.978 WhisperX Word-Level Coverage

Forced alignment across both WhisperX base and large-v3 models confirmed identical 0.978 word-level VAD coverage, demonstrating high consistency across spoken words.

[05]

Non-Word Region Acoustic Dynamics

Non-word VAD scores stabilized at 0.718–0.719 across both WhisperX models, indicating that acoustic regions between words contain distinct acoustic dynamics that differ from active speech.

[06]

Model Alignment Differences (Base vs Large-v3)

The WhisperX base model extracted 1,877 words matching the SRT count, while large-v3 extracted 1,884 words with 76 missing words relative to the reference comparison.

[07]

Subtitle Gaps as an Operational Silence Heuristic

Subtitle gap extraction proved valuable for inspecting false positive speech detections in candidate silence regions, though it functions strictly as an operational proxy rather than acoustic ground-truth silence.
